# Phase 4 — Content Quality Review Findings

Methodology: each of the 8 non-English released locales (es, de, fr, it, pt, he, hi, nl) was reviewed independently by a separate LLM agent, working from that locale's bilingual comparison packet (`docs/i18n-content-audit/quality-review/{locale}-vs-en.md`, generated by `scripts/i18n-extract-en-content.mjs --quality-packets`) with no visibility into the other 7 agents' work. Each agent checked: factual mismatches (times, prices, counts, place names — should match English/Spanish exactly, never be "translated"), machine-translation artifacts, tone/register consistency, and untranslated leftovers.

**This is LLM-assisted review, not native-speaker certification — see the plan's Phase 4 honesty note.** Every finding below is tagged by the reviewing agent's own stated confidence; none should be read as a verified bug until a native or professional speaker confirms it. Where an agent verified a finding directly against source (not just the flattened review packet), that's noted — those are the highest-confidence items in this document.

## Fix status

Findings below were triaged and largely acted on in a follow-up fix pass. Tiering logic and the full list of changes live in the fix-implementation plan (not checked in — see session history); the short version:

- **Tier 1** (source-verified bugs or single-answer grammar rules) — **fixed**: the `flattenItemToText` script bug (cross-locale pattern #3 above), Spanish VillaCoral/VillaMar `seoDescription` (Highest-priority #1), Spanish amenity plural agreement (Highest-priority #2), Italian elision and "Caribe Sud" (Highest-priority #5), Dutch gender agreement / soft hyphen / Casa Rana's missing 2 paragraphs (Highest-priority #3 — fixed by adding the missing paragraphs, reusing Delfin's existing Dutch translation of the same English boilerplate, per explicit user direction to restore parity rather than leave Dutch as the outlier).
- **Tier 2** (clear replacement, not fully mechanical — spot-check with a native speaker still worthwhile) — **fixed**: Hebrew gender agreement on "dense jungle/forest" (Highest-priority #4, both occurrences), Hindi possessive-gender slip on "अनुरोध" (all 9 occurrences), Portuguese "2 A/C" amenity label, Portuguese "shuttle"/"snacks" leftovers, French "check-out" leftover in Areka, German "berichten" → "mitzuteilen" phrasing (Highest-priority #7, all 9 occurrences of the exact shared string), Dutch "wij hebben je gedekt" calque, Dutch "locals" leftover.
- **Tier 3** (register/dialect/word-choice judgment calls) — **not fixed**, see the dedicated section below.

Not fixed, and intentionally out of scope for this pass: Hebrew's tri-gender "Puerto Viejo" paragraph (Highest-priority #6) and the Discover-section-translated-separately pattern (cross-locale pattern #1) — both are Tier 3 judgment calls, detailed below.

## Cross-locale patterns

Worth flagging first because these emerged **independently** in multiple locale reviews — none of the 8 agents could see another's output, so convergent findings across unrelated languages are stronger evidence than any single agent's read.

1. **The Discover/homepage section reads like it was translated in a separate pass from listings/blog, in at least 4 locales.** French: apostrophe style shifts from straight `'` to typographic `'` starting exactly at Discover's third paragraph, nowhere else in the corpus. Hebrew: "Puerto Viejo" and "Cahuita" are spelled differently in Discover than everywhere else (132 vs. 3 occurrences), plus a different apostrophe-equivalent character (geresh vs. plain). Hindi: place names use different Devanagari transliterations in Discover than in listings/blog (e.g. "पुएर्तो वियेहो" vs. "प्वेर्तो वियेहो" for Puerto Viejo). Italian: register mixes tu/voi specifically within Discover, nowhere else. This is the single most consistent pattern across the whole review — worth a dedicated pass reconciling Discover's copy against the rest of each locale's terminology, rather than treating it as 4 separate small issues.
2. **Formal/informal register bleeds inconsistently in at least 2 locales, always around the same kind of content.** Spanish: formal "usted" appears in service/logistics sentences ("nuestro equipo se comunicará con usted...") scattered across otherwise-"tú" pages (Delfin, Geco, Rana, Tucano, the homepage), plus one full article (`cahuitaPark`) written entirely in voseo instead of the site's usual tuteo. Italian: the Discover section specifically mixes "tu" and "voi" mid-paragraph. Reads like multiple translators/passes with different defaults, never reconciled.
3. **A `false` literal leaked into the VillaCoral/VillaMar extracted text**, identically on the English and every locale side — independently flagged by the Portuguese, German, and Hindi reviewers, who correctly guessed it wasn't a real translation issue. Root-caused during fix planning: it was **this session's own Phase 4 extraction tooling**, not the site's content. `scripts/i18n-extract-en-content.mjs`'s `flattenItemToText` (written for the quality packets) was missing the same `{ text, trailingBreak: false }` paragraph-object special case `flattenNode` already had, so it fell through to a generic property walk that stringified the `trailingBreak: false` boolean as a phantom extra line. `src/i18n/content/listings.ts` itself was checked directly and is well-formed — no stray `false` in the actual source. **Fixed**: `flattenItemToText` now special-cases the paragraph-object shape, and all 8 quality packets were regenerated (confirmed zero `false`-only lines remain in any of them).
4. **Several findings are one shared string repeated across many properties**, which matters because fixing it once fixes every occurrence: German's "Zögern Sie nicht, uns davon zu berichten" (stiff register for "let us know," in all 10 listings), Hindi's gender slip on "अनुरोध" (in 9 of 10 listings), Spanish's "un popular destino" word-order calque (Geco/Pappagallo/Rana share it verbatim, while Areka/Delfin/Giulia/Plumeria have the more natural "un destino popular" for the same sentence).

## Highest-priority findings

Ranked by how directly verified they are and how much they matter (SEO-visible copy, or a real guest-facing gap) — not by locale.

### 1. Spanish: VillaCoral/VillaMar `seoDescription` is Casa Geco's *English* text, verified in source

**`src/i18n/content/listings.ts:1391-1393`** (VillaCoral) and **`:1564-1566`** (VillaMar) — both properties' Spanish `seoDescription` is byte-identical to Casa Geco's English `seoDescription`: *"Located in the heart of town, this house has space for up to 5 people and features a fully equipped kitchen, a bathroom, 2 A/C units, and a private parking lot."* This is doubly wrong — English text on a Spanish page, describing a 5-guest in-town house with one bathroom instead of the private-pool Playa Chiquita villa it's actually attached to. `seoDescription` typically feeds `<meta name="description">`, so this is publicly SEO-visible, not an internal label. Almost certainly a copy-paste error (one villa was likely cloned from the other, and both inherited Geco's placeholder). **Confidence: high — directly verified against source, not a stylistic read.**

### 2. Spanish: amenity plural-agreement bug mirrors an already-fixed English bug

**`src/utils/constants.ts:1600`** — Casa Delfin's Spanish amenity `name: "2 Baños Privado Equipado"`. "Baños" is plural but "Privado Equipado" stayed singular; should read "2 Baños Privados Equipados." Notably, this is the *same class* of bug Phase 1 already found and fixed on the English side (`'2 Private Equipped Bathroom'` → `'2 Private Equipped Bathrooms'`) — the Spanish equivalent never got the parallel fix. **Confidence: high — Spanish adjective-noun agreement is a hard rule.**

### 3. Dutch: Casa Rana is missing 2 of 9 paragraphs every other locale has

Verified with a script counting every `paragraphs` array across every locale/property in `listings.ts`: `Rana.en/es/fr/de/he/it/pt/hi.paragraphs` all have 9 entries; `Rana.nl.paragraphs` has 7. The agent independently ran the same check across the *entire* corpus (all 10 listings, all 10 blog articles, Discover) and confirmed this is the **only** paragraph-count discrepancy anywhere. The English source has an accidental near-duplicate pair (two paragraphs restating the same "beach access to Cocles" / "getting around Puerto Viejo" content in different phrasing) — every locale except Dutch translated both; Dutch only has the first. Plausibly a reasonable deliberate dedup (the English duplication reads like its own content bug), but it's an undocumented deviation from every other locale. **Confidence: high that the counts differ (code-verified); a judgment call on whether it needs fixing or is the *right* behavior that should also be applied to the other 7 locales.**

### 4. Hebrew: "dense jungle/forest" mistranslated with wrong gender, twice, independently

Rana listing: *"ביער גשם טרופי עבות"* and `twoDaysInPV`: *"והג'ונגל הטרופי העבות"* — עבות (feminine plural) doesn't agree with either candidate noun in either sentence (יער/ג'ונגל are masculine singular). Same wrong word, two unrelated articles — reads as a systematic mistranslation of that specific adjective. **Confidence: medium-high (gender/number agreement is rule-checkable without full fluency).**

### 5. Italian: "Caribe Sud" (Spanish) used instead of "Caraibi"/"caraibico" in 4 articles, 6 places

`src/i18n/content/blog.tsx:781,785` (gettingToGandoca), `:2043` (bestTimeToVisit), `:2561,2568,2603` (indigenousTravel — including a section heading), `:3802` (busHours). The correct Italian term is demonstrably known and used correctly *elsewhere in the same articles* (busHours uses "costa caraibica" twice, just not at line 3802), so this reads like a leftover from a different source pass, not a stylistic choice. **Confidence: high — the correct term's presence elsewhere in the same files rules out "this is just how this translator writes."**

### 6. Hebrew: same paragraph treats "Puerto Viejo" as three different genders

Rana listing: *"פוארטו ויחו **הוא** יעד..."* (masculine) → *"...פוארטו ויחו **הופכת**..."* (feminine) → *"...**שהופך** את פוארטו ויחו..."* (masculine again), all in one paragraph. **Confidence: medium-high.**

### 7. German: repeated stiff phrasing for "let us know" across all 10 listings

*"Zögern Sie nicht, uns davon zu berichten"* ("don't hesitate to report it to us") closes the special-request paragraph identically in all 10 listings. "Berichten" fits recounting/reporting more than a casual "just let us know" — "mitzuteilen" or "Bescheid zu geben" would be more natural. Because it's one shared string, a single fix covers all 10 pages. **Confidence: medium.**

## Full per-locale reports

### Spanish (es)

Reviewed all 10 listings, 10 blog articles, Discover, and the amenity table. **Factual mismatches**: one critical finding (VillaCoral/VillaMar `seoDescription`, see Highest-priority #1 above) plus a low-confidence unverifiable claim ("piscina de agua salada" / saltwater pool — not contradicted by English, just not confirmable from text alone); everything else checked out cleanly across all 10 properties and all 10 articles. **MT artifacts**: one clearly broken sentence in `tenHoursInPuerto` ("Con de google maps y los habitantes de la zona , nos dirigimos..." — missing word, lowercase proper noun, singular/plural verb mismatch); a cluster of 3 listings (Geco/Pappagallo/Rana) sharing more English-word-order-calqued phrasing ("un popular destino") than the rest of the site, which has the more natural "un destino popular" for the same sentence; two missing accents ("comodo"→cómodo, "turisticas"→turísticas); the amenity plural-agreement bug (Highest-priority #2). **Tone**: the most consistent finding — formal "usted" bleeding into "tú" copy in the same kind of service/logistics sentence across Delfin/Geco/Rana/Tucano/the homepage (5 independent instances), plus `cahuitaPark` written entirely in voseo while every other article uses tuteo. **Untranslated leftovers**: none beyond the already-documented "Pet-friendly" case. Also noted: Pappagallo and Tucano's Spanish intro paragraphs are byte-identical despite genuinely different English source text for each — looks like Tucano's Spanish was copy-pasted from Pappagallo's twin-apartment sibling rather than translated from its own English. **Overall**: genuinely independently-written (not word-for-word MT), factually solid apart from the one villa bug, but shows clear evidence of at least two translation passes of uneven quality and register.

### German (de)

Reviewed all 10 listings, 10 blog articles, Discover, and the amenity table. **Factual mismatches**: none found — exhaustively checked all check-in/out times (correct 24-hour conversion for all 10 properties), all guest/bedroom/bathroom/A-C/parking counts, all prices/distances/durations including a correctly-localized decimal comma (1.5 hours → 1,5 Stunden), and every bus-schedule time across 3 separate articles. **MT artifacts**: "trockene Tasche" for "dry bag" (watersports gear term) reads as "a bag that happens to be dry" rather than the intended waterproof dry-bag; the "berichten" phrasing (Highest-priority #7); a shared boilerplate sentence ("At night, the town comes alive...") repeats the "leb-" root three times across 9 of 10 listings in a way that reads over-literal, versus Rana's cleaner independent phrasing of the same English sentence; "Selbständiger Check-in" for "Self Check-in" is an unusual fit (normally describes a self-employed person/business, not a process). **Tone**: clean — formal "Sie" used with zero exceptions across all 20+ items, matching the file's own stated intent. **Untranslated leftovers**: none — every "leftover" is a proper noun a native translator would also leave alone. Also flagged (not a translation issue): the `false` literal artifact in VillaCoral/VillaMar, identical in both languages. **Overall**: notably high quality — correct case agreement, compound formation, capitalization throughout, at least one genuinely idiomatic expression rather than a calque ("die Seele baumeln lassen"), zero factual drift. The flagged items are all things a single native-speaker pass would catch quickly.

### French (fr)

Reviewed all 10 listings, 10 blog articles, Discover, and the amenity table. **Factual mismatches**: none found — all times, counts, prices, distances checked line-by-line against English including correct 24-hour conversion and correct `1h30`-style duration formatting (not a literal "1,5 heures"). **MT artifacts**: minimal — "liste de contrôle au check-out" in Areka leaves "check-out" untranslated mid-sentence where Giulia/Plumeria's identical English sentence renders fully in French; one minor embellishment ("aussi sûr que confortable" adds an unsupported comfort claim not in the English "small but safe Cessna"). Grammar/gender agreement and French-specific typography (guillemets, spacing before punctuation, 24-hour time) are all handled correctly and consistently — notably, things raw MT commonly gets wrong. **Tone**: formal "vous" used with no register-mixing found, but the *same* shared English boilerplate paragraph gets two different (both individually correct) French treatments depending on which listing it's in (Areka/Delfin/Geco vs. Pappagallo/Giulia/Plumeria), and word choice for "town" varies between "la ville" (listings) and "du village" (Discover). The Discover section also shifts apostrophe style (straight → typographic) starting at its third paragraph, nowhere else in the corpus — see Cross-locale pattern #1. **Untranslated leftovers**: the one "check-out" instance above; "Rice and Beans" kept in English/mixed-case as a dish name (plausibly deliberate, same treatment as "Fritto Misto"). **Overall**: reads as competently and idiomatically translated, zero factual mismatches, but shows clear signs of multiple translation/editing passes producing inconsistent (though each individually correct) phrasing for shared source text.

### Italian (it)

Reviewed all 10 listings, 10 blog articles, Discover, and the amenity table, cross-referenced against source for the actionable items. **Factual mismatches**: none — checked every time, count, distance, and price across all 20 long-form items, including correct decimal-comma localization (1.5 hours → 1,5 ore). **MT artifacts**: "Caribe Sud" (Spanish, not Italian) instead of "Caraibi"/"caraibico" in 4 articles, 6 places (Highest-priority #5); a missing elision in "dalla interior designer" (should be "dall'interior designer") repeated identically in both VillaCoral and VillaMar's shared sentence; minor inconsistent A/C terminology across 4 amenity-table entries (aria condizionata / climatizzatori / unità di aria condizionata for near-identical English source strings). **Tone**: the Discover/homepage section mixes informal "tu" (used everywhere else on the site, 21 of 21 other items) with formal "voi" mid-paragraph — including "Lavori" (tu) sitting directly between two "voi" forms in the same sentence block; also minor USD-vs-spelled-out currency inconsistency between two articles. **Untranslated leftovers**: none rising above deliberate style choices (Rice and Beans, WiFi capitalization variance — the latter inherited from the English source itself, not introduced by Italian). **Overall**: solid and factually airtight against English on every checkable detail; the "Caribe Sud" finding is the single most concrete, fixable issue since the correct term is demonstrably already used correctly elsewhere in the same content.

### Portuguese (pt)

Reviewed all 10 listings, 10 blog articles, Discover, and the amenity table. **Factual mismatches**: none — all times (including correct 24-hour and decimal-comma conversions), counts, and prices checked across all 20 items. **MT artifacts**: "2 A/C" → "2 Ar condicionado" is awkward (a near-identical string, "2 A/C Units," correctly becomes "2 unidades de ar condicionado" elsewhere in the same table); three SEO titles read slightly redundant translating "Home" as "Casa" next to an already-present "Casa [Property]" ("Casa Delfines - Aluguer de Casa de Férias..."); "reef-safe sunscreen" is translated two different ways across two articles (one adds "biodegradável," not in the English). **Tone**: mostly consistent formal-but-warm register; one Discover badge heading ("Os preços mais baixos") breaks from its three sibling badges' terser, article-free style. **European vs. Brazilian consistency — a positive, explicitly checked finding**: consistently European Portuguese throughout (casa de banho, autocarro, duche, pequeno-almoço, Caraíbas, the "a + infinitive" construction, correct EU-PT accented preterites), no Brazilian forms mixed in anywhere. **Untranslated leftovers**: "shuttle" (twice) and "snacks" (once) slip through in some occurrences while being correctly translated ("transporte privado," "lanches") elsewhere in the same or nearby articles — inconsistent rather than a wholesale gap. Also flagged: the shared `false` literal artifact. **Overall**: fluent, natively-phrased European Portuguese, zero factual drift, with narrow and easily fixable gaps (two specific words that leaked through in some but not all occurrences, one awkward amenity label).

### Hebrew (he)

Reviewed all 10 listings, 10 blog articles, Discover, and all 16 amenity rows — raw text only, no bidi/RTL rendering check (that's Phase 6's job). Explicitly lower confidence than the Latin-script reviews, per the plan's own instruction to scrutinize Hebrew more. **Factual mismatches**: none — every time, count, price, and distance checked across all 20 items, including the harder case of numeral-noun gender agreement (שני vs. שתי), which was handled correctly everywhere checked. **MT artifacts**: the "dense jungle/forest" gender-agreement bug, twice independently (Highest-priority #4); the "Puerto Viejo" tri-gender paragraph (Highest-priority #6); amenity-table terminology ("חדר אמבטיה") doesn't match listing-body terminology ("חדר רחצה") for the same word, bathroom; a stray hyphen incorrectly inserted before a Hebrew word in one of two otherwise-parallel bakery mentions; several lower-confidence nits (a possible transliteration drop in "Braulio Carrillo," "זוהרת" for "pristine" reads as "glowing/radiant" rather than "spotlessly clean," a doubled possessive, one calqued compound adjective). **Tone**: mostly consistent; minor אנחנו/אנו (informal/formal "we") wobble in 5 scattered places; "100Mbps" kept as a Latin abbreviation in listings/amenities but spelled out in Hebrew in Discover — another data point for the Discover-separate-pass pattern. **Untranslated leftovers**: none — English retained only for proper nouns/brands/URLs, consistent with the other locales' treatment of the same terms; no garbled or reversed character order found anywhere in the raw text. **Overall**: substantially better than bare-minimum MT (idiomatic phrases used correctly and consistently, the harder gender-agreement case handled right almost everywhere), but with one real recurring grammar bug and a Discover-section-specific cluster of spelling/punctuation drift. The agent was explicit that its confidence here is materially lower than for Romance/Germanic languages.

### Hindi (hi)

Reviewed all 10 listings, 10 blog articles, Discover, and the amenity table. Explicitly lower confidence than the Latin-script reviews, per the plan's own instruction. **Factual mismatches**: none — every time (with correct AM/PM-equivalent markers), count, price, and distance checked across all 20 items, plus a positive finding: a shared boilerplate sentence about bathroom count correctly varies ("दोनों बाथरूम," both bathrooms) only for the actually-2-bathroom properties. **MT artifacts**: a recurring possessive-gender slip on "अनुरोध" (request) across 9 of 10 listings (Highest-priority pattern, see cross-locale #4) — notably, the *same sentence* gets a different, correctly-gendered possessive a few words later for a different noun, suggesting a specific, narrow error rather than general carelessness; minor semantic narrowing ("rays" → specifically "stingray"); mild repetition of "स्थित" (located) twice in one sentence, shared across 2 listings. A positive signal: correct ergative-object verb agreement in a case naive MT often gets wrong. **Tone**: formal आप used consistently everywhere, no informal तुम slippage found; the Discover section uses different Devanagari transliterations for the same proper nouns (Puerto Viejo, Manzanillo, Cahuita, Punta Uva, Playa Chiquita, Cocles) than listings/blog use — the single most visible finding in this review, verifiable at the character level without fluency, and the strongest instance of the cross-locale Discover-separate-pass pattern; "Pet friendly" is rendered 3 different ways across the site as a related instance of the same drift. **Untranslated leftovers**: none — brand/business names consistently kept in Latin script as a deliberate, sensible choice. Also flagged: the `false` artifact, and a "Deelite"/"Deleite" spelling variance that originates in the English source itself, not the Hindi. **Overall**: genuinely competent, native-reading translation with consistent formal register and zero factual drift; the gender-agreement finding is a one-line fix with wide impact if a native speaker confirms it, and the Discover transliteration drift is the clearest, most visible issue in this locale.

### Dutch (nl)

Reviewed all 10 listings, 10 blog articles, Discover, and the amenity table, with source cross-checks (including a script counting paragraph arrays across the whole corpus) for structural findings. **Factual mismatches**: none in the usual sense — but the Casa Rana missing-2-paragraphs finding (Highest-priority #3), confirmed as the *only* such discrepancy anywhere in the corpus. **MT artifacts**: "wij hebben je gedekt" is a literal calque of "we've got you covered" that reads as "we've pinned you down" in Dutch, not the intended reassurance; two gender-agreement errors ("het terminal"/"het busterminal" should be "de," "zwaar bagage" should be "zware bagage"); one relative clause that doesn't parse cleanly; "privaat" used once where "privé" is used consistently everywhere else on the site (20+ other occurrences); an invisible Unicode soft hyphen (U+00AD) inside "jungle­paden" in one `seoDescription`, verified by scanning the raw source for that exact code point — the only such character anywhere in the three content files; several minor internal terminology inconsistencies (eigen/privé split across amenity labels, "Outside Parking" breaking the site's usual modifier-first compounding pattern, inconsistent Bribri- hyphenation within one article). **Tone**: a real strength — informal "je/jij" used with zero exceptions across all 20+ items, and a nice detail showing careful (not mechanical) translation: `bestTimeToVisit`'s first-person-singular voice (unique among the articles) is correctly preserved rather than flattened to the site's usual "we." **Untranslated leftovers**: "locals" left in English twice in one article's SEO title/heading, inconsistent with "local(s)" being correctly translated everywhere else in the corpus. **Overall**: a genuinely well-done, noticeably-above-raw-MT translation — correct compound formation, localized idioms rather than calques, uniform register, zero factual drift on anything checked. The Rana paragraph gap is the most concrete, actionable finding and is a structural completeness question as much as a translation one.

## Tier 3 — not fixed, needs a native speaker

These are register/dialect/word-choice judgment calls, not mechanical corrections — an LLM picking a "fix" here risks confidently replacing one unverified guess with another, which is exactly what this phase's own honesty note warns against. Left as-is, flagged for a native or professional speaker:

- **Spanish usted/tú mixing** — formal "usted" bleeding into otherwise-"tú" service/logistics sentences across Delfin/Geco/Rana/Tucano/the homepage (5 instances), plus `cahuitaPark` written entirely in voseo while every other article uses tuteo. Whether to standardize on one register, and which, is a judgment call.
- **Italian tu/voi mixing in Discover** — informal "tu" (used in 21 of 21 other items) mixes with formal "voi" mid-paragraph, including one "tu" form sitting directly between two "voi" forms in the same sentence block.
- **German word choices** — "trockene Tasche" for "dry bag" (watersports term; reads as "a bag that happens to be dry" rather than the waterproof-gear term) and "Selbständiger Check-in" for "Self Check-in" (normally describes a self-employed person/business, not a process). Plausible fixes exist but weren't applied without a native check.
- **Hebrew amenity-table terminology** — "חדר אמבטיה" in the amenity table vs. "חדר רחצה" in listing body text for the same word, bathroom. Also the tri-gender "Puerto Viejo" paragraph in the Rana listing (Highest-priority #6) — masculine, then feminine, then masculine again in one paragraph; picking which gender to standardize on is exactly the kind of call this pass shouldn't make alone.
- **Hindi Discover-section transliteration drift** — 6+ place names (Puerto Viejo, Manzanillo, Cahuita, Punta Uva, Playa Chiquita, Cocles) use different Devanagari transliterations in Discover than in listings/blog. Real risk of introducing a new inconsistency while "fixing" an old one without a native speaker choosing the canonical form.
- **The Discover-section-translated-separately pattern** (cross-locale pattern #1 above, independently flagged in French, Hebrew, Hindi, and Italian) — a diagnosis, not a single fix. Properly reconciling it means a full retranslation pass of Discover per locale against each locale's own established listings/blog terminology; out of scope for this round.
- **All remaining "low confidence" nits** each per-locale report already flagged as such inline (e.g. Spanish's "un popular destino" calque, Portuguese's redundant "Casa" in SEO titles and inconsistent "reef-safe sunscreen" translation, Hebrew's אנחנו/אנו wobble, Dutch's "privaat"/"privé" split and amenity eigen/privé inconsistency) — see the per-locale sections above for the full list.

## What this phase does not tell you

Per the plan's own framing, this is a flagged-for-review list assembled by 8 independent LLM readings, not a certified translation sign-off. Every finding above should be verified by a native or professional speaker before anyone changes source content on the strength of it alone — treat the "highest-priority" ranking as "worth a human's time first," not "confirmed bugs in priority order." The two source-verified structural findings (Spanish `seoDescription`, Dutch Rana paragraph count) are the exceptions — those are checkable facts about the codebase, not translation-quality judgment calls, and don't need a linguist to confirm.
